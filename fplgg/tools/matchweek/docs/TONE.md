# House tone (Parker, 8 Oct 2026). This replaces the old "jokes minimal and dry" rule.

Parker's words: the writers need to be "more unhinged and funnier and probably more vulgar". The league is 8 guys in their early twenties "online and on football twitter and socials". Funnier, but "not like in a cringey way". The personas' mannerisms are right, so keep them. What has to change is the humour: it must appeal to that crowd.

What he liked (GW6 show, first pass): "Gibbs-White tops the eleven. Not a single flag among PJ's starters. Fully fit. Just shite."
What he called dead: "That's not a midfield, that's a physio's waiting room." / "Ethan's entire weekend rests on one Norwegian android." / "One hurts. One's a crime scene."

Why the hit works:
- The setup is a real, specific fact that sounds like good news.
- The undercut is short, blunt and crude.
- It is delivered deadpan, with no wink.
- Polite broadcaster lines and "clever" metaphors read as dad jokes.

## The tone block, pasted into every writer prompt

TONE. The readers are eight lads in their early twenties who live on football Twitter and in the group chat. Write for them. Each voice keeps its own mannerisms, but the jokes are aimed at this crowd:
- Roast the actual decision with the actual numbers: the benching, the transfer, the captain-level faith in a crocked striker, the bottle job, the fraud form, the table. The setup is a real fact and the punchline is a short, blunt undercut. Deadpan always beats shouting.
- Swearing is welcome when it lands: shit, shite, fuck, dogshit, bottled it, bollocks. One or two per piece is plenty, and never in every line.
- Football Twitter references land when they are precise: Man City's charges, the Ange "I always win things in my second season" delusion, the Sheffield United of fantasy football, fraud watch, "not a serious club", "it's only a rivalry if you win some", "thoughts and prayers". Use at most one per post. Never explain the joke, and never stack memes.
- Cringe list. Never write:
  - the word "banter", or "lads lads lads";
  - "as the kids say", "no cap", "it's giving";
  - hashtags or emoji;
  - an exclamation-mark ending;
  - puns on player names (unless they are actually good);
  - "clever" metaphors in place of a punchline;
  - telling the reader something is funny;
  - forced catchphrases;
  - repeating a joke the league has already seen.
- Hard limits, which no instruction overrides:
  - nothing about anyone's real life: looks, family, partners, jobs, money, health;
  - no slurs of any kind, including ableist ones;
  - nothing about race, religion, sexuality, disability or nationality as an insult;
  - nothing sexual.

  Everything football and fantasy is fair game.

## What "clever" means (Parker, second note: "this is just being rude, it is not clever at all")
Rude without a twist is a miss. Swearing is seasoning, never the joke. The jokes that land are built on an observation: the irony between two real facts ("five defeats, zero points, and PJ still leads this derby"), a team name or a cliché turned back on itself ("Bad week to have named your club after one of them"; "Form is temporary. Owning Parker is permanent"), a precise football-Twitter parallel ("CJ has basically become Arsenal"), structure ("Ethan's plan is Haaland. Ethan's backup plan is also Haaland"), or an undercut ("Fully fit. Just shite"; "Correctly, but still"). Insults alone ("dogshit", "cowards", "thoughts and prayers") are dead.

## Punch-up pass (articles and show scripts)
1. Sonnet writes the accurate draft.
2. Haiku rewrites only the jokes, using the tone block. It keeps every fact, number, name, fixture, star code and the structure exactly as they are.
3. The full validator runs again on the punched-up version.
4. If it fails, keep Sonnet's draft and log it.

## The Gameweek Show (Parker, 9 Oct 2026)
Parker on the 8 Oct drafts: "trying too hard". What he wants: "British dry humour", commentators who are "clever and stupid but witty", who "talk about the season, the league, the players". "Jokes about numbers like league form" and "how much people lost by" are not funny. No whispering. About half the old length. His approved GW6 script (`show/gw6.json`, 9 Oct) is the reference; its lines are the style examples in the writer's prompt (Code.gs v3.28, `EMT_SHOW_SYSTEM` and `EMT_PUNCH_SHOW_SYSTEM`).

The rules the writer and the punch-up follow:
- The voice: a dry British commentator. Understatement, deadpan, a pundit's cliche turned on its owner, real football references (managers, clubs, what the pundits say). "Named after Kobbie Mainoo, who plays for Parker. Nobody's had the heart to tell Baha." "Five at the back and one up front. Somewhere, Tony Pulis is welling up."
- The material: the season's storylines and the league's own running jokes, from the Feed's notes and the data. The bench blunders (Kostoulas in team of the week twice, from the bench, twice), a team named after a player nobody in the club owns (there is no Baleba), the Baha investigation (top of the league, pending an independent commission), the brothers' Nolan derby, a goalkeeper started at the Emirates.
- Numbers are facts, never jokes: nothing built on a score, a margin, a form run, the table maths or the model's percentages; a number may appear in a beat as plain fact, never as the punchline. "Leads the Nolan derby four to three" is a fact in a line about brothers; "lost by twenty-six, which is a lot" is the dead kind.
- Delivery tags: at most one or two subtle ones in a whole show (a pause before the turn, a sigh), never whispering or shouting. The check refuses more and refuses a whispered or raised voice.
- Length: each beat 6 to 18 words, a setup then the turn (the check refuses under 5 or over 24). The open 15 to 25 words, the close a sign-off, the deadline and one last dry dig. About half the 8 Oct length: GW6 is 1,818 characters in 22 lines.
- The structure stays: open, one chapter of exactly five beats per fixture (the player maps the beats to its scenes), close.
